Digital Transformation

Digital transformation is using new digital technologies to create or modify business processes, culture, and customer experiences. Google Cloud enables this through three transformation clouds: data, open, and collaboration.

Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)

You manage OS and apps; Google manages hardware. Example: Compute Engine.

Platform as a Service (PaaS)

You deploy code; Google manages the runtime. Example: App Engine, Cloud Run.

Software as a Service (SaaS)

Fully managed app you just use. Example: Google Workspace.

💡 Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

Moving to cloud shifts spending from capital expenditure (CapEx, buying hardware upfront) to operational expenditure (OpEx, paying for what you use). Know this distinction.

Infrastructure & Modernization

Compute options

  • Compute Engine - VMs (IaaS)
  • Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) - managed containers
  • Cloud Run - serverless containers
  • App Engine - serverless apps (PaaS)
  • Cloud Functions - event-driven functions

Modernization paths

  • Rehost ("lift and shift") - move as-is to VMs
  • Replatform - minor tweaks, e.g. managed DB
  • Refactor - re-architect to cloud-native
  • Hybrid / multicloud - Anthos / GKE Enterprise

Security & Operations

🔐 Shared Responsibility

Google secures the infrastructure (security OF the cloud); you secure your data, identities, and configuration (security IN the cloud). Google also promotes shared fate - actively helping customers be secure.

Products You Must Recognize

CDL is scenario-based: given a business need, pick the right product. Memorize the "need → product" mappings in the tables above. Focus on BigQuery, Vertex AI, GKE, Cloud Run, Cloud SQL, Spanner, Pub/Sub, and IAM.
